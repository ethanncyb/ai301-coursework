# Rubric: is this plan ready to post and build from?

Seven required checks and one preferred. Each one judges the plan's substance
against the evidence it claims to rest on, never the shape of the write-up: a
three-line plan that names its cause, its boundary and an observable test is
ready, and a sectioned one-pager whose cause the evidence does not support is
not.

In eval mode the bundle is the whole world: the issue context, the repo-facts
block, the repro-evidence block, the candidate plan, and the candidate plan
comment. In live mode the drafts are the candidate side, the reproduction
evidence is the student's posted repro comment on that issue, and the
issue-side evidence comes from the locations named in
`references/evidence-guide.md`.

## Checks

| Check | Evidence | Pass condition | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| cause-follows-evidence | The plan's stated cause or diagnosis, read against what the repro-evidence block actually shows: the behavior it pins down, the artifacts it carries, and the conditions under which it observed them. Evidence guide: Diagnosis. | The cause is **supported by** the shown evidence: every element of it is either visible in the evidence, cited from the issue or thread, or reasoned from the evidence in a way the plan states. A mechanism one layer below what the artifacts can see — internal code behavior inferred from black-box output — is supported when the plan both names it as grounded and says what grounds it: the control runs that narrow it, the regression window that dates it, or the thread analysis it matches. Black-box evidence does not bar a code-level diagnosis; an ungrounded one does. A cause narrower than the evidence passes — a plan may localise one of several reproduced symptoms and fix only that, so long as it says so. Fail if the cause asserts a mechanism the evidence does not reach, contradicts what the artifacts show, or names a different behavior than the one reproduced. Fail also when the plan targets a symptom the evidence shows is downstream of a cause the same evidence localises, with no stated reason for stopping there: treating the visible effect while the evidence points one layer deeper is the failure this check exists to catch. Absent repro evidence is not a free pass: with nothing shown, a confident cause is unsupported and fails. | required |
| change-bounded | The plan's scope pair — what it states it will change and what it states it will not. Read against the issue's own ask. Evidence guide: Scope. | Both of: (a) **a boundary exists** — the plan says what it is not doing, in its own words, whether as an explicit exclusion, a deferral to another issue, or a named limit on the change; (b) **the work sits inside the issue's ask** — every element of the change serves the issue, or the plan says why a neighbouring change rides along. File count is not the measure: a three-file change with a stated exclusion is bounded, and a one-file change that nowhere says where it stops is not. Fail an unbounded change (refactors, renames, cleanups, or version bumps picked up along the way with no stated reason), a change that addresses a different issue's surface without saying so, and a plan whose scope a reader could only discover by reading the diff. | required |
| plan-executable | The named files, functions, or areas and the first concrete action, read as a stranger who holds this plan, the issue, and the repository, and nothing else. Evidence guide: Executability. | A stranger could begin the first step without asking a question whose answer only the author has. The change is located — named files, functions, config keys, or an area specific enough that one search finds it — and the approach says what will be done there, not merely that something will. Where a plan names the area but defers the exact function, it passes if it states a method of locating it that is already in hand (a debug trace it has working, a failing test that points there, a named call path to follow); deferring the precise site in a large codebase is honest sequencing, not vagueness, when the route to it is stated. Fail "refactor the handler", "fix the parser", "add validation" and other statements of intent with no target; fail a plan whose first step depends on a decision the plan never makes; fail a location named so broadly that the reader must pick the file themselves. Terseness is not a failure: one sentence naming a function and the edit to make in it is executable where three paragraphs of intent are not. | required |
| test-plan-observable | The plan's test plan, read against the repro evidence's steps and the behavior the issue describes. Evidence guide: Verification. | All of: (a) **re-runs the proof** — the test plan exercises the behavior the repro evidence established, through the real code path, not a restatement that the change will work; (b) **states the expected-after** — it says what will be observed once the change lands, in terms someone could check: a value, a message, an exit status, a rendered state, a test that moves from failing to passing; (c) **the observation would actually distinguish** the fixed state from the current one. Fail a test plan that only asserts the suite still passes, one that names no observable outcome ("verify it works", "confirm the fix"), and one whose check would read the same before and after the change. A plan that cannot re-run the original repro passes on (a) by routing the same inputs through the real path instead, where it says so. | required |
| confidence-matches-evidence | Every assertion in the plan and the comment about cause, mechanism, scope of effect, or outcome, set against what the evidence carries. Evidence guide: Honesty. | Stated certainty tracks actual support: what the evidence shows is asserted plainly, what is inferred is marked as inference, and what is unknown is named as unknown rather than omitted. Support is not only a direct artifact. A finding cited from the issue or thread, a mechanism narrowed by control runs, and a regression window that dates a change all carry an assertion, and a plan that names which of these it rests on has met this check for that assertion. What fails is the unsourced confident sentence, not the confident sentence whose source is named. Unknowns that bear on the approach are stated — an open question to a maintainer, a condition not yet tested, a dependency whose behavior is assumed. Fail a plan that asserts an untraced cause as fact ("obviously", "clearly the problem is", "this will fix it"), one that promises an outcome the test plan cannot show, one that widens a single-environment result to platforms or versions untested, and one whose risks section is confident where the evidence is thin. Naming an uncertainty is never itself a failure: a plan that says "I have not checked whether anything downstream depends on this" is stronger than one that does not ask. | required |
| comment-carries-the-plan | The candidate plan comment, read against the plan it accompanies and the thread highlights. Evidence guide: Comms. | All of: (a) **it states the plan** — a reader of the comment alone learns the diagnosis and the intended change, not merely that a plan exists; (b) **it is this issue's** — it names something true of this issue in particular, so it could not be pasted onto another; (c) **it engages the thread** — where a maintainer or commenter has already proposed a direction, asked a question, or ruled something out, the comment adopts it, answers it, or says plainly why not. Fail a comment that points at an attachment or elsewhere without stating the plan, a piggyback ("same approach as above", "agree with the above plan"), a bare claim of intent with no approach, and a comment that proceeds past a maintainer's stated direction without acknowledging it. An open question the thread never answered may be decided by the comment, provided the comment says which way it went. | required |
| conventions-respected | The repo-facts `contribution policy` line and the repo's stated asks about contributions and comments, read against both the plan and the comment. Live mode: `CONTRIBUTING.md` in the repo root, `docs/`, or `.github/`, any AI-policy file it links, and the issue and PR templates. Evidence guide: Comms. | Every requirement the repo actually states and the plan can meet at planning time is met: a required branch-naming shape is followed where the plan names a branch, a stated test or check gate is reflected in the test plan, a declared process step is not skipped, and where the policy requires AI usage to be disclosed, at least one comment names the assistance and its extent. Two things pass: a policy silent on AI, and a policy that welcomes or conditions AI use without asking for disclosure. Requirements scoped to pull requests do not bind a plan comment, except where the plan states it will skip one. Fail only a requirement the repo states and the package does not meet. | required |
| alternative-considered | The plan, for an approach it rejected and the reason it gave. Evidence guide: Scope. | The plan names at least one other way it could have gone and why it did not go that way — a simpler change it judged insufficient, a larger one it judged out of scope, or a direction the thread raised and it declined. Ranks only: a plan whose single approach is well-grounded is ready to post without one. | preferred |

## Verdict rule

Accept if and only if all seven required checks pass. Any single required fail
rejects the package: a plan is a commitment made in public, and one broken
element of it misdirects both the maintainer who reads it and the build that
follows. `unclear` counts as fail — a plan a reader cannot verify from what it
contains is not ready to post, and in eval mode evidence absent from the bundle
is absent.

`alternative-considered` is preferred and never changes a verdict. Report its
grade, and among plans that are ready, prefer the ones that show their work on
the approach not taken.

Where the package contains no repro evidence at all, `cause-follows-evidence`
and `test-plan-observable` are graded against that absence rather than excused
by it: an unevidenced diagnosis fails, and a test plan with no established
behavior to re-run fails on clause (a).
